Control coverage

Execution success is not settlement correctness.

A transaction can execute successfully while chain, asset, sender, receiver, amount, finality, uniqueness, or canonical-asset controls independently identify a problem.

Execution Chain Asset Sender Receiver Amount Finality Instruction uniqueness Transfer uniqueness Canonical asset
01 Consistency ≠ validity

Expected and observed data can match while the asset itself is not the approved canonical asset.

02 Missing evidence ≠ failure

Required evidence that is unavailable produces an explicit UNKNOWN rather than an invented conclusion.

03 Control finding ≠ business disposition

The orchestrator preserves findings; a separate policy layer would determine HOLD, REVIEW or REJECT.

Operating standard

Claims should survive review.

The standard across these projects is deliberately conservative: preserve evidence, show limitations, and do not convert uncertainty into a stronger claim than the data supports.

01

Public evidence only

No client data, production settlement data, credentials, or private operational information in public work.

02

No fake completeness

Unavailable evidence remains unavailable. Missing data is never silently replaced with synthetic facts.

03

Independent controls

A failure in one control does not rewrite the evidence produced by another independent control.

04

Explicit limitations

Engineering MVPs and research prototypes are described as such rather than presented as production systems.
